25 Professional Tanning & Wellness Tips

Aligned with New Sunshine Brand Training · Updated for 2026 · By Isabella Roberts

Important: Isabella Roberts is not a licensed medical professional or dermatologist. All tips are based on 6+ years of professional tanning industry experience and New Sunshine brand training. Nothing in this guide constitutes medical advice. Always consult a licensed healthcare professional before beginning any new skincare routine, particularly if you have existing skin conditions, sensitivities, or are taking medications that may affect UV sensitivity. Results vary by individual skin type and routine adherence.

🛏️ Bed Tanning

01

Know Your Fitzpatrick Skin Type Before You Start

The Fitzpatrick Scale classifies skin into six types based on how it responds to UV exposure. Type I never tans and always burns. Type II usually burns and tans minimally. Type III sometimes burns and tans uniformly. Type IV burns occasionally and always tans. Types V and VI rarely or never burn. Your Fitzpatrick type determines your recommended session length, starting frequency, and which lotion categories are appropriate for you. Beginning without knowing your skin type is the most common reason new tanners burn or plateau quickly. Ask your salon to assess your type at your first visit or use the quiz inside Luminate Glow to identify it.

🛏️ Bed Tanning
02

Build a Base Before Using Bronzers

New tanners should spend the first 2–4 weeks using an intensifier only — no bronzers. Intensifiers contain Tyrosine, which stimulates melanin production naturally and allows the skin to develop its darkest inherent pigment without any added color. Jumping straight to a DHA bronzer on unprepared skin results in uneven color, faster fading, and an unreliable baseline. A solid base makes every subsequent product work significantly better and produces more consistent, longer-lasting results.

🛏️ Bed Tanning · Beginner
03

Never Tan Without Lotion

Dry skin reflects UV light rather than absorbing it. Tanning without lotion requires significantly more UV exposure to achieve the same color result — which means more time in the bed, greater UV accumulation, and color that fades faster because the skin's surface is not conditioned to retain it. Even an entry-level intensifier dramatically improves color development, evenness, and longevity. Skipping lotion to save money costs more in session time and results.

🛏️ Bed Tanning · All Levels
04

Consistency Produces Better Results Than Long Sessions

The skin's melanin production process has a saturation point per session — beyond which additional UV exposure does not produce more color, only more UV accumulation. Shorter, more frequent sessions (3–5 times per week depending on your Fitzpatrick type) produce deeper, more even color than fewer long sessions. The skin needs time between sessions to complete the melanin production cycle. Overexposure in a single session does not accelerate results — it increases the risk of a burn and causes uneven fading.

🛏️ Bed Tanning · All Levels
05

Use a Tan Extender Daily Between Sessions

A tan extender used daily at home is one of the highest-impact steps in any tanning routine. Products like the Australian Gold Hemp Nation Tan Extender line contain DermaDark™, which continues building color gradually between UV sessions without any additional UV exposure. Combined with the HydraRich Blend that locks in moisture, consistent daily use can extend color retention by days and visibly deepen your overall result over time. Apply after showering while skin is still slightly damp for best absorption.

🛏️ Bed Tanning · All Levels
06

Don't Shower for at Least 4 Hours After a DHA Bronzer

DHA (dihydroxyacetone) is a sugar cane extract that chemically darkens the proteins in the outermost layer of the skin. This reaction takes time to develop — typically 2–4 hours post-session and up to 8 hours for full development. Showering before the DHA has fully processed rinses away a significant portion of your color development. Wait a minimum of 4 hours after any session using a delayed bronzer before your first rinse. When you do shower, use lukewarm water — hot water accelerates fading.

🛏️ Bed Tanning · DHA Bronzer
07

Wash Hands Immediately After Applying DHA Bronzer

DHA reacts with the proteins in any skin it contacts — including your palms. Failure to wash hands immediately after applying a DHA bronzer results in darkened palms that are difficult to remove. Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water within 30 seconds of application. The same applies to any area you do not intend to color — cuticles, nails, and the webbing between fingers are particularly prone to buildup.

🛏️ Bed Tanning · DHA Bronzer
08

How and When to Use a Plateau Breaker

A tanning plateau occurs when the skin stops progressing in color despite consistent sessions and routine maintenance. This typically happens because the skin has reached its natural melanin saturation point with the current product and approach. Plateau breakers — such as the California Tan Coast Optimizer — contain the highest concentration of instant and delayed bronzers along with specific technologies like CuO2 and TRF designed to re-stimulate color development. Use a plateau breaker 1–2 times per week maximum alongside your regular lotion — not as a daily replacement. Overuse diminishes the effect and does not accelerate results.

🛏️ Bed Tanning · Advanced
09

Tingle Lotions — What They Are and Who They're For

Tingle and sizzle lotions contain Benzyl Nicotinate, which increases microcirculation in the skin — producing a warming, reddening, tingling sensation that drives deeper color development by oxygenating skin cells during the session. They are formulated exclusively for experienced tanners who have a well-established base tan. They are not appropriate for new tanners, fair or sensitive skin types, or anyone who has never used a tingle product before. Protocol: always try a sample packet before purchasing a full bottle. Never apply tingle products to the face. Tanning beds must be cleaned twice after any tingle lotion use. Redness after a tingle session is normal and typically subsides within a few hours.

🛏️ Bed Tanning · Experienced Only
10

Long-Term Tanner Skin Repair Protocol

Skin that has been tanning consistently for years develops specific needs that standard routines do not address. Chronic UV exposure gradually depletes collagen, increases skin protein cross-linking, and can reduce the skin's ability to produce even, uniform melanin. Long-term tanners should incorporate: daily tan extenders with DermaDark™ for color maintenance, a collagen-supporting lotion such as Australian Gold Magnetize or Peptide Pro for its amino acid and collagen/elastin blend, red light therapy sessions 3–4 times per week to support cellular collagen production, and a Vitamin C and Hyaluronic Acid serum (such as California Tan Restored) applied post-session for antioxidant protection and hydration. Plateau breakers should be used strategically — weekly rather than as a reflexive response to stalled color.

🛏️ Bed Tanning · Long-Term Tanner
11

Medications That Affect UV Sensitivity

Certain medications increase the skin's photosensitivity — meaning a shorter UV session than usual can cause a burn reaction in someone taking them. These include some antibiotics (particularly tetracyclines and fluoroquinolones), some acne treatments (including retinoids and doxycycline), certain antihistamines, some antidepressants, and some hormonal medications including certain birth control formulations. If you are taking any prescription medication, check the label for photosensitivity warnings or consult your prescribing healthcare provider before UV tanning. This is not a reason to avoid tanning entirely — it is a reason to know your current medication list and adjust session times accordingly.

🛏️ Bed Tanning · All Levels
12

Understanding the Tanning Triangle

A tanning triangle is a targeted tanning approach used to concentrate UV exposure on specific body areas — most commonly the stomach and lower body — by positioning the body at an angle within the bed to maximize UV contact with those zones. It's a technique used by tanners who want to even out color discrepancies between areas or intensify results on naturally harder-to-tan zones. Proper positioning, appropriate lotion selection for that skin area's sensitivity, and not extending session time beyond your normal duration are all critical when using this method. It should only be used by experienced tanners with an established base.

🛏️ Bed Tanning · Advanced

✨ Spray Tan

13

Shave or Wax at Least 24 Hours Before — Never Same Day

Shaving opens the hair follicle and temporarily sensitizes the skin's surface. Spray tanning immediately after shaving causes the solution to absorb unevenly into the open follicles, creating a dotted or speckled appearance — particularly visible on the legs. Shave or wax at least 24 hours before your appointment to allow the follicles to close and the skin to normalize. If you shave the morning of your appointment, you will likely see visible follicle stippling in the final result.

✨ Spray Tan
14

No Deodorant, Perfume, or Oil-Based Products Day Of

Deodorant creates a chemical barrier on the underarm skin that blocks DHA absorption — resulting in a pale, patchy underarm area in an otherwise bronzed spray tan. Perfume contains alcohol and other compounds that can interfere with even color development. Oil-based body products — including many daily moisturizers — coat the skin surface and prevent the spray solution from contacting the skin proteins it needs to bond with. Arrive to your appointment with clean, dry, product-free skin from the neck down.

✨ Spray Tan
15

Wait at Least 8 Hours Before Your First Rinse

The DHA in spray tan solution requires time to complete its chemical reaction with skin proteins. Most professional spray solutions are formulated for a minimum 6–8 hour development window, with deeper, richer results achieved at the 8–12 hour mark. Rinsing early cuts off the reaction before full color develops. The immediate post-spray color you see is largely cosmetic guide color — the true DHA result appears in the hours after application. If applying in the evening, sleeping in the spray and rinsing in the morning produces the best results for most solutions.

✨ Spray Tan
16

Pat Dry — Never Rub — After Rinsing

Rubbing the skin with a towel after rinsing a spray tan physically removes color from the surface before it has fully set. Pat the skin dry gently with a soft towel using a pressing motion rather than dragging. The same principle applies to towel drying after any shower during the life of your spray tan — aggressive rubbing accelerates fading and creates uneven wear patterns, particularly across shoulders, hips, and any area subject to clothing friction.

✨ Spray Tan
17

Switch to a Glycerin-Based Body Wash to Extend Your Tan

Standard body washes containing sulfates (sodium lauryl sulfate, sodium laureth sulfate) act as surfactants that strip the skin's surface — including spray tan color — with each use. Glycerin-based, tan-extending body washes such as the Australian Gold Hemp Nation Body Wash line bind water to the skin surface and protect the color layer from being stripped. Switching to a tan-extending body wash from the moment of your first rinse through the life of your spray tan can add 3–5 days to your result without any other changes.

✨ Spray Tan
18

Wear Loose, Dark Clothing Immediately After Your Session

Tight clothing worn immediately after a spray tan creates two problems: friction rubs off the cosmetic guide color before the DHA has set, and the fabric can absorb the solution and create transfer marks. Wear loose, dark-colored clothing to your appointment and for at least 8 hours post-session. Avoid bras with underwire, waistbands, socks, and shoes immediately after — these are the areas most commonly affected by transfer and pressure lines in a freshly applied spray tan.

✨ Spray Tan
19

Why DHA Doesn't Mix With Spray Tan Pre-Prep

The skin only has a limited number of surface proteins for DHA to bond with per session. If you use a DHA bronzer in a UV bed before a spray tan — and that DHA is absorbed during tanning — those protein binding sites are already partially occupied when the spray solution is applied. The result is reduced spray tan color intensity and faster fading. When combining UV bed tanning and spray tanning in the same visit, use only a Double or Triple Dip certified silicone-free lotion — which contains no DHA — in the bed. This preserves the full binding capacity of the skin for the spray solution.

✨ Spray Tan

💡 Red Light Therapy

20

Red Light Therapy After UV Tanning Is the Better Sequence

Red light therapy's primary mechanism is anti-inflammatory and regenerative — it supports cellular repair, collagen production, and recovery. UV tanning triggers controlled UV-induced inflammation as part of the melanin production process. Doing red light therapy after a UV session allows the red light to support the skin's post-UV repair process, reduce inflammation, and assist collagen restoration — counteracting some of the cumulative UV effects on collagen over time. Red light before UV tanning may slightly increase circulation and nutrient delivery to the skin, but the post-UV application has stronger clinical support for skin health outcomes. Importantly: red light therapy does not fade your tan.

💡 Red Light Therapy
21

Use Only Pu(red)™ Compatible Lotions in Red Light Sessions

Standard tanning lotions contain silicone, which forms a surface barrier on the skin. In red light therapy, this barrier restricts the penetration of red and near-infrared wavelengths — the mechanism through which the treatment works. Lotions formulated with California Tan's Pu(red)™ Technology are specifically engineered to allow unobstructed wavelength transmission. If you don't have access to Pu(red)™ products, the Australian Gold Peptide Pro Hybrid Facial is a hydrogel-based formula that does not obstruct red light. A plain aloe vera gel with no silicone is also a safe minimal option. Never use a standard tanning lotion in a red light session and expect full results.

💡 Red Light Therapy
22

Tracking Mood and Energy Is Part of Your Red Light Protocol

Red light therapy's benefits extend well beyond skin appearance. Consistent sessions support mitochondrial ATP production in skin and muscle cells, have been shown to support serotonin levels (relevant to mood and seasonal affective patterns), and can improve sleep quality through circadian rhythm regulation. These results are cumulative and often subtle in the early weeks. Tracking your mood, energy, and sleep quality before and after sessions — even informally — allows you to see the progression that skin-only observation misses. 💡 Red Light Therapy
23

Apply Post-Treatment Products Immediately After Your Session

The skin is in an active regenerative state immediately following red light therapy — cellular activity, collagen synthesis signaling, and blood flow are all elevated. Applying targeted serums and post-treatment products within the first few minutes after a session — while this window is open — maximizes absorption and ingredient delivery. Tattoo Protection — What It Does and When You Need It

UV light fades tattoo ink over time by breaking down the pigment particles embedded in the dermis. UV-induced oxidative stress causes the pigment to degrade and migrate, resulting in color loss and blurring of fine lines over repeated exposure. Tanning lotions with Tattoo Technology — such as the ColorGuard™ complex in Australian Gold's Adrenaline Rush, or the Anti-Fade Tattoo Protection in Designer Skin's Command — contain ingredients that help counteract this oxidation and provide a moisture seal over the ink. They do not eliminate UV-related tattoo fading entirely, but consistent use measurably slows the rate of fading compared to untreated UV exposure. JWOWW's Ink-Drink™ Complex is specifically formulated for this purpose and is effective for both UV tanning and spray tan aftercare.
